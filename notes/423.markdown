## Books

This week I read:

- [The Left Hand of Darkness][] by Ursula K. Le Guin

  This came up in conversation, so I decided to re-read this.  The thing that
  really stood out to me is that the ideas of gender and sexuality seem
  incredibly dated now, even though this is obviously a very feminist story.  We
  certainly haven't conquered sexism as a society, far from it, but I don't
  think if this novel were written today the protagonist would comment on how he
  distrusts the feminine nature of the people of Gethen, or make comments like
  "he had a girl's quick delicacy in his looks and movements, but no girl could
  keep so grim a silence as he did".  Yes, the alien nature of the world and
  difficulty the protagonist has adapting to it (and how weirded out he is at
  the end by the arrival of his companions from the ship, thrusting him once
  more into a world of binary sex) *is the point*, but I just feel it would be
  less overt if written today.  We have nonbinary people on Earth now.

  I do like the gradual shift we get over the book from Genly wanting the world
  to welcome the Ekumen before he will call the ship, to calling the ship
  because it is the right thing to do on a personal level for Estraven, who is
  just one man.  It mirrors the visit from Estraven's kemmer-mate who calls
  Genly's mission immoral because Genly says it is greater than any one man or
  any personal obligations.  Sometimes it is worth changing a whole world just
  to (try to) save one man.

[The Left Hand of Darkness]: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Left_Hand_of_Darkness
