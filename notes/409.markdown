## Books

This week I read:

- [The Two Towers][] by J. R. R. Tolkien

  Many more differences to the films that I'd forgotten and, like Fellowship,
  some instances where pivotal moments are treated almost casually, resolved in
  very little text compared to everything around them.  For instance, when
  Gandalf heals Theoden in the film we see that Saruman is magically controlling
  (or at least influencing) Theoden and there's that cool line, "I draw you,
  Saruman, as poison is drawn from a wound."  In the book, we don't see a
  *direct* involvement from Saruman, only the *indirect* involvement via
  Wormtongue, and the healing of Theoden is a much simpler and less magical
  matter.

  The treatment of the Ents is, I think, better in the book.  In the film the
  Ents decide not to go to war, but Merry and Pippin trick Treebeard into
  approaching Isengard and being so outraged by the devastation that he sees red
  and calls the Ents, who come marching out of the trees just a moment later as
  if they'd been waiting there all along.  But in the book Treebeard wants war
  and the Ents decide in favour.

  Two major differences, of course, are how Sam and Faramir are treated.
  There's no trip to Osgiliath in the book, Faramir considers the matter and
  decides he wants no part in this Ring business, setting them free to go about
  their quest.  And Gollum does not trick Frodo into banishing Sam, the two
  remain fast friends throughout.

  Onto the Return of the King!

[The Two Towers]: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Two_Towers


## Roleplaying Games

### Hot Springs Island

There's a new Swordfish Islands book: [Shipwrecked on the Swordfish Islands][]!
I had a quick look through and it's good, once I've read through it properly
I'll bring it into our campaign.  I'm planning to put several of the missing
nereids on other islands, and I'm also going to situate the [Scenic
Dunnsmouth][] somewhere with the Time Cube being a way to free Meltalia from the
time-loop.

But enough about that, we also played today.  Sadly we had to cut the session
short, but in the time we played the players returned to the Lapis Observatory
and started exploring it in earnest, as the translations of some brochures
they'd taken last time were now done, promising sights such as the "Conservatory
of Extraplanar Delights" and the "Observatory Museum".  There's also a magic
sword I put in that tower some sessions ago which I'm hoping they'll find, as
they don't have any magic weapons yet.

They're not sure yet what to do about Svarku, but the mood seems to be turning
towards getting in contact with the Night Axe ogres, which will be fun.  I hope
they find Svarku's missing gem, as that'll *really* get things going.

It's hard to predict how long this campaign will last as it *could* go on for
quite some time, if the players are determined to explore things carefully and
thoroughly, but there's also several objects and individuals scattered around
that, if disturbed, could radically alter the status quo.  I expect the campaign
to come to a sudden fiery end just when least expected.

## Miscellaneous

I found myself in the unfortunate position this week of needing to buy a new USB
C hub, as my current one sometimes disconnects randomly.

I'm not sure if it's just me, but it seems weirdly difficult to get a good one.
And by good I mean that it has several ports and also supports USB C Power
Delivery (so you can also use it to charge a phone or something).  Everything
that supports Power Delivery has at most 4 ports (and usually fewer).  Similar
difficulties finding hubs that support USB C Video.  I don't need to transmit
video over USB C, but it's *weird* that there aren't really any hubs that
support that.

Is there something in the spec that makes a fully-featured USB C hub just
difficult to produce?

I ended up buying a 7-port hub but it doesn't support Video or Power Delivery.
I can just keep plugging my phone into the wall I guess.

And if you try upgrading to the next generation, by getting a Thunderbolt hub,
those seem limited by spec to only support 4 connections (one of which will be
used to plug it into your PC).  Again, it feels *weird* that these three things
are simultaneously true: (1) almost every new device is USB C; (2) desktop
motherboards commonly only have two or three USB C ports; (3) fully-featured USB
C hubs have maybe four (if you're lucky) additional ports.
