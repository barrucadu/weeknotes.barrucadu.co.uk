## Books

This week I read:

- [Glamorama][] by Bret Easton Ellis

  This was kind of like a combination of American Psycho (also by Bret Easton
  Ellis) and Fight Club.  It takes the shallow appearance-obsessed culture of
  American Psycho and dials it up to 11, while also bringing in the weird
  irreality and delusions of Fight Club.  The book follows Victor Ward, a model,
  but people keep seeing him at places and events he doesn't remember going to.
  He gets caught up in a missing person case and is sent to London by a
  mysterious figure to go find them, and while there gets taken advantage of and
  caught up in a terrorist cell composed of other models and actors.  But it's
  really unclear how much of this is real.

  The book is from Victor's point of view, and he describes everything as a
  "set", in terms of "scenes", and frequently talks to "the director" and
  interacts with "PAs".  Are those people actually there?  A lot of the time it
  seems like he might be talking to himself or imagining a conversation, but at
  other times it seems like there is someone there.  There are two competing
  "film crews", and one of the "directors" is confused and concerned by this
  whenever Victor brings it up: is this him subconsciously realising that
  something is very wrong about the situation he finds himself in?  At the end
  he disappears into a witness protection programme, or is abducted (one of the
  two), and when he calls his sister for help *he himself answers the phone* and
  says "hey man, whoever you are, it'd be cool if you'd stop bothering my
  sister."  So who is the main character?

  The whole book is an exercise in the dissolution of the self, of identity,
  into a sea of shallow people and their appearance-obsessed concerns.

[Glamorama]: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glamorama


## Roleplaying Games

### Traveller

We kicked off our Secrets of the Ancients campaign this week.  We spent
basically the whole session on character creation: we ended up with three great
characters and one person clearly destined to be a henchman, or perhaps a goon,
all of whom have strong connections to Vlen Backett and the other important NPCs
of the campaign.

I ended the session giving them the hook for the first adventure.  We're doing
one short adventure first before getting into the campaign proper, as I didn't
want to spend a whole session emphasising how good a friend Vlen Backett is and
also how mysterious he is only to then say "Right, characters done, let's start
playing!  You receive notice of Vlen Backett's death."  So we opened the game in
Regina Highport, with their ship in the docks for the next two months getting
some extensive changes (they rolled multiple ship benefits in character
creation, which we turned into one lab ship + a few turrets and some software
upgrades), where they received a message from Vlen asking if they were looking
for work, as he had a wanna-be noble in a crisis.  This then turned into the
opening of *Marches Adventure 5 - Safari Ship*, which I moved to be closer to
Regina, and swapped out the corporate quest-giver for Vlen.  I had the NPC
patron thank Vlen for helping him out "yet again" and joking "I hope you don't
intend to call all these favours in at once!", dropping another hint than Vlen
does lead a strange life and has unexpected contacts.

And there we ended.

We'll pick up in two weeks time with the adventure proper, and I'll start with a
quick recap covering how all of them know Vlen (we decided the broad strokes in
character creation, but I'll come up with some detail) and the mission they now
find themselves on.  The hope is that they start to wonder what Vlen's deal is,
because then when they complete the mission (which will be fun in itself) and
return to Regina the news of his sudden death will hit harder than if they'd
*never* interacted with him as a character.
