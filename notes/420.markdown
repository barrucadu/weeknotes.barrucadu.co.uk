## Books

This week I read:

- [Inanna][] by Kim Echlin

  I decided to re-read this to see if there was anything I could draw upon for
  my Bronze Age worldbuilding project (see below) but, eh, not really.  It's
  pretty simplistic, and the translations take some significant linguistic
  liberties in ways that I don't think actually improve the stories.  It's ok as
  an introduction, but I've read much better stuff by now.

- Volume 14 of [Frieren: Beyond Journey's End][] by Kanehito Yamada and Tsukasa Abe

  Had this one sitting on the shelf for a while and totally forgot I had it.
  The story has definitely changed focus.  Early on, it was very much focussed
  on Frieren and how she experiences the passage of time differently to the
  shorter lived races, but gradually it's widened in scope to being more
  generally about the old world order being left behind.  The current story arc
  is about a plan to assassinate Seerie, one of the three great elven mages
  (Frieren is one of the others, and the third is said to have died somewhat
  recently).  It is still good, it's definitely not suffering from the same
  power- and scope-creep that *That Time I Got Reincarnated As A Slime* is, but
  I did prefer it earlier on, when the stakes were more personal.

## Roleplaying Games

### Pioneer

This week I ran the adventure from the Pioneer rulebook, *Rescue at Low Earth
Orbit*, in which the players are called for an emergency rescue mission.  A
tourist vessel has been struck by space debris which damaged its heat shield and
vented most of its atmosphere: they can't return to Earth, and they only have
enough oxygen for 36 hours.

Despite being mechanically almost identical to Traveller, it felt very different
in practice.  We focussed on the little details so much more: the countdown
before takeoff, adjusting the ship's trajectory to avoid its own set of space
debris, performing a spacewalk to fix a bent actuator, performing a Hohmann
transfer between orbits, and so on.  And at the end no gratitude, just a
grilling by the oversight committee probing into all the decisions they made and
questioning how even minor mishaps could have been foreseen and prevented.

I could imagine running this more in the future.

### Worldbuilding

I started building my Bronze Age setting this week.  I've decided to focus on
the kingdom of Larsa, which is in the south by the Persian Gulf, for now.  [The
Lions of Tell Arn][] will be nearby Isin, as it's suffered from a period of
decline and has been recently conquered which feels like a good fit for the city
of Kurhan in the module.  I've made up Akkadian names for the other important
locations in Tell Arn:

- Makkalet, a settlement that sees a lot of trade: Tamkār, "merchant"
- Lambsblood, a small village near the Tell: Dāmimrum, "lambsblood"
- Tell Arn: Tīl Qubūrī, "Mound of Graves"

and the real historical city of Uruk will fill the role of the nearby town of
Isa.

[Let Us Build a Tower][] will, obviously, be in Babylon.  I haven't thought
about what else is going on there yet, but I have some sources to read.

I did some research into trade routes too.  I couldn't find a good map, but I
could find where goods come from, and so inferred how they would arrive in
Mesopotamia.  From the Persian Gulf I have copper, ivory, pearls, and
semiprecious stones; from Egypt, precious metals; from both Elam and the Levant,
wood; and from Afghanistan (via Elam), lapis lazuli, gold, and tin.
International trade was a huge deal in the Bronze Age and I want it to be a big
part of my setting.

I'm wondering if I can draw ideas from Ultraviolet Grasslands or Traveller for a
trader campaign, treating the setting as a pointcrawl with overland and river
routes between cities and other points of interest.  I'm not really sure what
the "default action" of the setting will be, but that's very important to figure
out.
