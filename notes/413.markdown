## Books

This week I read:

- [Robot][] by Adam Wiśniewski-Snerg

  This was a weird book.  The protagonist is a humanoid robot, indistinguishable
  from a real human (and, as we later learn, a perfect physical copy of a real
  person) sent to infiltrate a huge underground shelter and carry out the orders
  of its creator, "the Mechanism".  Except, the Mechanism doesn't seem to convey
  any orders at all.  The first third of the story might be a dream or
  hallucination (I suspect there's symbolism here that would be more clear if I
  were to go back and re-read it).  Characters reoccur but have no memory of
  meeting the protagonist previously, or think he's somebody else.  The book is
  about alien abduction, but more than that it's about the limits of perception
  and imagination, but even more than that it's about free will and what that
  means.

[Robot]: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam_Wi%C5%9Bniewski-Snerg


## Roleplaying Games

### Hot Springs Island

A player who had been absent for the last few sessions returned this week, so we
started with a long recap.  Then straight back to exploration.  The players
ended up finding the Temple of Tranquility and accepting a bet to go down to the
third basement level, but ran away when they attracted the attention of the
giant albino centipede.  They want to go back some time, but not to win a bet,
for *revenge.*

Much discussion was had about getting a ship so they can travel between the
islands without needing to rely on the Martel schedule, but no decisions were
made yet.  I'll prepare things just in case.

Finally, they decided to go make friends with the ogres.  We ended the session
with them a couple of hexes away from Glavrok Village.  Despite encountering a
load of boars and other animals with parts the ogres value (and which the
players *know* the ogres value), they haven't killed anything to bring as a
peace offering.  So I'm interested to see what their approach will be.


## Miscellaneous

[Hister][] is pretty cool.

I started using it like [bookmarks][], manually adding stuff to the index that I
thought I'd want to return to in the future, but that's a lot of effort and I've
come around to appreciate using the browser extension to just add every page I
visit and using exclusion rules to filter out things I *don't* want.  I have 987
pages[^987] indexed so far, with 55 rules (most of them excluding an entire
domain, but it allows arbitrary regex).  I've also chosen to exclude forum-like
sites (reddit, hackernews, lobsters) from the index so in practice a lot of
things I read are never going to get indexed, but I chose to do that because
these sites have a *lot* of pages, and I barely ever want to return to, say, an
old reddit thread.

I've got it set as my default search engine in Firefox, with fallback to Google
if it can't find something[^mn].  It's working well!

[^987]: That includes all 412 (as of the time of writing this) weeknotes, so
  I've visited around 575 unique pages this week that were interesting enough to
  not immediately blacklist from the index.

[^mn]: I tried using [Marginalia][] as my fallback search engine, but it's sadly
  just not good enough.  Almost everything I searched Marginalia for I had to go
  back to Google.

[Hister]: https://hister.org/
[bookmarks]: https://github.com/barrucadu/bookmarks
[Marginalia]: https://marginalia-search.com/
