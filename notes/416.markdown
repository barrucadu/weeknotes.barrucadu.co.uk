## Books

This week I read:

- [Dangerous Games][] by Joseph P. Laycock

  Seth Skorkowsky did [an amazing video on this book][] which covers the three
  waves of RPG moral panics in the USA: the first wave was that players would
  lose the ability to distinguish between reality and fiction; the second was
  that by imagining demons and gods players were actually opening themselves to
  real occult influence (i.e., the Satanic Panic); the third that these games
  glamorised and encouraged murder and other immoral behaviour.

  But it's not just a dry history, the author constructs two very compelling and
  well-researched arguments.  Firstly, he ties these into the wider cultural
  fears at the time to explain *why* these specific fears rose up and why
  roleplaying games were the target.  Secondly, throughout the book he refers to
  "frames" of truth, arguing that the post-Enlightenment world, and particularly
  Protestantism, have been so highly suspicious of imagination, collapsing all
  the ways in which something could be "true" or "meaningful" into two
  modes---backed up by empirical evidence, or totally meaningless
  speculation---resulted in a culture poorly-equipped to deal with highly
  imaginative games.

  I read most of this while on trains and I intend to go back through it and
  take notes, as it's really well written and has a lot of references I want to
  look into.

- [Last Days][] by Brian Evenson

  Not to be confused with *Last Days* by Adam Nevill, which is also a horror
  novel about a weird cult.  I suppose it's a good title for religious horror.
  I picked this up after enjoying Brian Evenson's short story collection *A
  Collapse of Horses* and this novel apparently started as a short story that
  got expanded.  It's about a weird self-mutilation cult called "The Brotherhood
  of Mutilation" that draw in a guy who lost his hand, wanting to use him for
  their own ends.  It opens with Matthew 5:29--30:

  > And if thy right eye offend thee, pluck it out, and cast it from thee... And
  > if thy right hand offend thee, cut it off, and cast it from thee.

  It's basically an action movie in book form, it's horror-themed but definitely
  not written with the intend to instil fear.  But despite that it's great, I
  suppose you could call it a thriller.

## Miscellaneous

This weekend I attended [VeXpo][], the UK VTuber convention.  I've never been
before, I wasn't even into VTubers this time last year, but now I am, and I
decided to check it out.  It was a lot of fun.

With the Mollieverse timing, this did unfortunately mean I had to cart around a
bunch more stuff with me, which was fairly inconvenient.  I had to be at
St. Pancras for the Eurostar at 05:30 on Thursday so I stayed in London
overnight on Wednesday, meaning I had my work laptop, personal laptop, dirty
clothes from Wednesday, changes of clothes for Thursday to Monday, etc.  I was
tempted to skip Mollieverse---these big company gatherings, they're not that
fun---but foolishly, despite knowing the VeXpo dates months in advance, I hadn't
booked the Friday off work, and attending Mollieverse was *strongly encouraged*
so I felt bad about booking it off on relatively short notice.  A lesson for
next time I suppose.

Anyway, I wasn't really sure what to expect.  While I do watch VTubers, I almost
exclusively watch tiny indie VTubers, so I only knew a couple of names amongst
all the meet & greets, panels, and performances, and they weren't people I'd
watched.  In retrospect, I should probably have just booked a couple of meet &
greets at random to see what's what.  The panels were fun, that's where I spent
most of the time.  I missed the concerts due to poor ticket planning on my part
(they sell out very fast!) so another lesson for next year.  I met a small
VTuber that I follow and she gave me a sticker, and I saw a few others that I'm
kind of aware of but don't watch around the con.  There were some very good
cosplays too.  Overall, it was a good time, and I'll be back next year.

[VeXpo]: https://vexpo.uk/
